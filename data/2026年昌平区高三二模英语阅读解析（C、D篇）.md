## 阅读 C 篇

### 【原文逐句对照与考点解析】

Imagine you’re **<font color="red">in line at</font>** your favourite bakery, deciding whether to have a doughnut or a tart. 
<font color="lightgrey">想象一下，你正在你最喜欢的面包店**排队**，决定是吃甜甜圈还是挞。</font>

You **<font color="red">weigh them up</font>**, the doughnut wins, and you **<font color="red">settle on</font>** that. 
<font color="lightgrey">你**权衡它们**，甜甜圈赢了，你**决定**选那个。</font>

By the time you’re at the front of the line, however, only tarts are left. So, you buy one. 
<font color="lightgrey">然而，当你排到队伍最前面时，只剩下挞了。所以，你买了一个。</font>

These two decisions feel completely different. 
<font color="lightgrey">这两次决定感觉完全不同。</font>

One is a free decision that involves consideration **<font color="red">based on</font>** your personal preferences, while the other is a forced decision that involves simply recognizing and picking the only available option. 
<font color="lightgrey">一个是**基于**你的个人偏好的自由决定，涉及权衡；而另一个是强迫决定，仅涉及识别并挑选唯一可用的选项。</font>

Neuroscientists have long assumed free decisions **<font color="red">rely on</font>** different processes in the brain compared to forced decisions. 
<font color="lightgrey">神经科学家长期以来一直认为，与强迫决定相比，自由决定**依赖于**大脑中不同的过程。</font>

But our latest research shows our brains actually make these decisions in surprisingly similar ways. 
<font color="lightgrey">但我们的最新研究表明，我们的大脑实际上以惊人相似的方式做出这些决定。</font>

Decades of research have shown that, to make decisions, our brains gradually gather evidence for each option **<font color="red">over time</font>**. 
<font color="lightgrey">数十年的研究表明，为了做出决定，我们的大脑会**随着时间的推移**逐渐为每个选项收集证据。</font>

By measuring electrical brain activity, researchers have identified a brain signal that reflects this accumulation of evidence during simple decisions. 
<font color="lightgrey">通过测量大脑电活动，研究人员已经确定了一种大脑信号，它反映了简单决策过程中证据的积累。</font>

Like a loading bar building to 100%, the signal gradually rises to a particular level before a decision is made. 
<font color="lightgrey">就像进度条加载到100%一样，信号在做出决定之前逐渐上升到特定水平。</font>

Rather than climbing steadily towards one option, the signal fluctuates back and forth between the alternatives. 
<font color="lightgrey">信号并不是稳步向一个选项攀升，而是在备选项之间来回波动。</font>

This partly explains why we aren’t always consistent with our choices, even when our preferences are stable. 
<font color="lightgrey">这部分解释了为什么即使我们的偏好是稳定的，我们的选择也并不总是前后一致。</font>

This signal has been identified for forced decisions. But what about open-ended choices? 
<font color="lightgrey">这种信号已经在强迫决定中被确定。但开放式选择呢？</font>

To answer this question, we recorded people’s brain activity while they chose between sets of coloured balloons. 
<font color="lightgrey">为了回答这个问题，我们记录了人们在不同颜色的气球组之间进行选择时的大脑活动。</font>

They viewed either two balloons of different colours to freely choose between, or a single balloon they were forced to pick. 
<font color="lightgrey">他们要么看到两个不同颜色的气球供自由选择，要么看到一个他们被迫挑选的单个气球。</font>

For both free and forced decisions, the brain activity **<font color="red">unfolded in</font>** a strikingly similar way. 
<font color="lightgrey">对于自由决定和强迫决定，大脑活动都以惊人相似的方式**展开**。</font>

The signal rose steadily to the same peak level before a choice was made, climbing faster for quick choices and slower for longer ones. 
<font color="lightgrey">在做出选择之前，信号稳步上升到相同的峰值水平，快速选择时上升较快，耗时较长的选择时上升较慢。</font>

One might assume the brain forms free and forced decisions similarly, suggesting decision-making may be more automatic than it feels. 
<font color="lightgrey">人们可能会认为大脑以相似的方式形成自由和强迫决定，这表明决策可能比感觉上更自动化。</font>

This **<font color="red">echoes</font>** Benjamin Libet’s experiments in the 1980s, which show brain activity begins ramping up before people are even consciously aware of their intention to act. 
<font color="lightgrey">这**呼应了**本杰明·利贝特在20世纪80年代的实验，该实验表明在大脑活动开始增强时，人们甚至还没有意识到自己的行动意图。</font>

But while the process may be automatic, what the brain is accumulating tells a different story. 
<font color="lightgrey">但是，虽然过程可能是自动的，但大脑积累的内容却说明了不同的情况。</font>

The evidence it weighs up is **<font color="red">drawn entirely from</font>** who you are—your preferences, your goals, your experiences. 
<font color="lightgrey">它权衡的证据**完全源于**你是谁——你的偏好、你的目标、你的经历。</font>

Two people may **<font color="red">go through</font>** the same neural process and land on the same choice, and yet arrive there for completely different reasons. 
<font color="lightgrey">两个人可能**经历**相同的神经过程并做出相同的选择，但到达那里的原因却完全不同。</font>

So rather than asking whether our choices are truly free, perhaps the better question is what it really means for a choice to be yours. 
<font color="lightgrey">因此，与其询问我们的选择是否真正自由，或许更好的问题是，一个选择属于你究竟意味着什么。</font>

And the next time you find yourself **<font color="red">in line at</font>** the bakery, know that your brain has already been quietly gathering evidence toward your baked good of choice, and that choice happens a little faster than you realise. 
<font color="lightgrey">下次当你发现自己在面包店**排队**时，请记住，你的大脑已经一直在悄悄地为你选择的烘焙食品收集证据，而且那个选择发生得比你意识到的要快一点。</font>

### 【题目详细解析】

**28. Why does the author mention “a loading bar” in Paragraph 3?**
<font color="lightgrey">作者为什么在第3段提到“进度条”？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：Like a loading bar building to 100%, the signal gradually rises to a particular level before a decision is made. (Para. 3)</font>

*   **A. To explain why choices are inconsistent.**
    *   **错误原因**：张冠李戴。虽然第3段提到了选择不一致（inconsistent），但那是由于信号波动（fluctuates）引起的，而非进度条类比的目的。
*   **B. To prove the required level for decisions.**
    *   **错误原因**：表述不准。进度条侧重于描述证据积累的“过程”，而非仅仅为了证明“水平”。
*   **C. To highlight the speed of decision-making.**
    *   **错误原因**：以偏概全。速度只是过程的一个属性，进度条更多是描述证据积累的动态过程。
*   **D. To illustrate how evidence is gradually built.**
    *   **正确依据**：原文用“loading bar building to 100%”来类比“accumulation of evidence”（证据的积累），生动地说明了大脑在做决定前是如何逐渐收集和构建证据的。

**29. What can we learn from this passage?**
<font color="lightgrey">从这篇文章中我们可以学到什么？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：The evidence it weighs up is drawn entirely from who you are—your preferences, your goals, your experiences. (Para. 5)</font>

*   **A. Stable preferences reduce choice changes.**
    *   **错误原因**：与原文相悖。原文说“even when our preferences are stable”，我们的选择也并不总是前后一致（not always consistent）。
*   **B. Personal identity helps make your choices.**
    *   **正确依据**：原文指出大脑权衡的证据“is drawn entirely from who you are—your preferences, your goals, your experiences”（完全源于你是谁——你的偏好、目标、经历）。这说明个人身份（你是谁）在决策中起到了核心作用。
*   **C. The brain reacts to a decision at the last moment.**
    *   **错误原因**：与原文相悖。原文提到大脑活动在人们意识到意图之前就已经开始增强了（begins ramping up before... consciously aware）。
*   **D. Free and forced decisions vary in brain processes.**
    *   **错误原因**：与原文相悖。文章的核心发现是两者的大脑活动“unfolded in a strikingly similar way”（以惊人相似的方式展开）。

**30. What is the logical chain of this passage?**
<font color="lightgrey">这篇文章的逻辑链条是什么？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">核心逻辑：文章先设定了一个面包店选食品的场景（Situation），接着提出了传统的假设（Assumption：自由和强迫决定过程不同），然后描述了研究人员进行的实验（Experiment：气球选择实验），最后得出了结论（Conclusion：两者大脑过程相似，但证据源于个人）。</font>

*   **A. Problem→Theory→Test→Solution**
    *   **错误原因**：文章并非在解决一个实际问题（Problem），而是在探讨一个科学现象。
*   **B. Phenomenon→Cause→Theory→Outcome**
    *   **错误原因**：文章结构并非典型的因果分析。
*   **C. Background→Review→Research→Suggestion**
    *   **错误原因**：文章最后并非给出建议（Suggestion），而是科学结论和哲学思考。
*   **D. Situation→Assumption→Experiment→Conclusion**
    *   **正确依据**：文章从生活情境出发，挑战旧假设，通过实验验证，得出新结论。这完美契合了文章的写作思路。

## 阅读 D 篇

### 【原文逐句对照与考点解析】

When was the last time you **<font color="red">raced against</font>** an unforgiving clock? 
<font color="lightgrey">你上一次与无情的时间**赛跑**是什么时候？</font>

Perhaps you skipped breakfast, **<font color="red">shelled out for</font>** a taxi or missed time with your family. 
<font color="lightgrey">也许你没吃早餐，**花大钱**打车，或者错过了与家人相处的时间。</font>

Many of us have become slaves to time, with huge portions of our day spent chasing appointments and deadlines. 
<font color="lightgrey">我们中的许多人已经成了时间的奴隶，一天中的大部分时间都花在赶预约和截止日期上。</font>

But what is this thing we’re trying to beat? 
<font color="lightgrey">但我们试图战胜的这个东西究竟是什么？</font>

We tend to imagine time as constant and non-negotiable, ticking by somewhere out in the world, impossible to slow or stop. 
<font color="lightgrey">我们倾向于将时间想象成恒定且不可协商的，在世界某个地方滴答流逝，无法减速或停止。</font>

Yet an emerging scientific picture is that such “clock time” isn’t a physical phenomenon. 
<font color="lightgrey">然而，一个新兴的科学图景是，这种“时钟时间”并不是一种物理现象。</font>

It’s a mathematical tool useful for coordinating our interactions, but with no independent existence of its own. 
<font color="lightgrey">它是一种用于协调我们互动的数学工具，但它本身并没有独立的存在。</font>

As with other key innovations, such as money, we can no longer **<font color="red">get by without</font>** it. 
<font color="lightgrey">就像金钱等其他关键创新一样，我们已经无法**离开**它而**生存**。</font>

But disproving the myth of the clock can help us to **<font color="red">focus on</font>** how life really progresses. 
<font color="lightgrey">但破除时钟的神话可以帮助我们**专注于**生活真正是如何进展的。</font>

Psychologists have identified the rise of “time famine”, where the more efficient and productive we try to be, the more precisely we measure time, and the more we try to **<font color="red">pack into</font>** our busy schedules, the less time we actually feel we have. 
<font color="lightgrey">心理学家已经确定了“时间饥荒”的兴起，即我们越是努力追求效率和多产，我们就越精确地衡量时间，我们越是努力向忙碌的日程中**塞进**更多内容，我们实际上感觉拥有的时间就越少。</font>

This has a big impact on quality of life: people experiencing time famine are less likely to do things they enjoy, or help each other. 
<font color="lightgrey">这对生活质量有很大影响：经历时间饥荒的人不太可能去做他们喜欢的事情，或者互相帮助。</font>

Time is puzzling in other ways, too. 
<font color="lightgrey">时间在其他方面也令人困惑。</font>

We have no sensory organs for detecting it, nor any dedicated brain areas for tracking it. 
<font color="lightgrey">我们没有探测它的感觉器官，也没有专门的大脑区域来追踪它。</font>

Our experience of time can vary hugely: minutes drag if we’re bored; hours race if we’re excited. 
<font color="lightgrey">我们对时间的体验可能会有巨大的差异：如果我们感到无聊，几分钟也会显得漫长；如果我们感到兴奋，几小时也会飞逝。</font>

And in certain circumstances, our sense of time can even go in circles, break apart or stop altogether. 
<font color="lightgrey">在某些情况下，我们的时间感甚至会循环、破碎或完全停止。</font>

Take Lara, who suffers from a condition called motion blindness. 
<font color="lightgrey">以患有一种被称为运动盲症的Lara为例。</font>

When she pours tea, the liquid appears as a frozen column in the air, before suddenly overflowing the cup. 
<font color="lightgrey">当她倒茶时，液体看起来像空气中冻结的柱子，然后突然从杯子里溢出来。</font>

Rather than being mere mistakes, these effects reflect something deeper: the role we all play in creating our own time. 
<font color="lightgrey">这些效应不仅仅是错误，它们反映了更深层的东西：我们所有人在创造属于我们自己的时间中所扮演的角色。</font>

Because even scientists don’t find any moving river of time; most believe there’s no physical flow of events beyond our perception, no moment of “happening” or “becoming” in which the future slips into the past. 
<font color="lightgrey">因为即使是科学家也没有发现任何流动的时间之河；大多数人认为，除了我们的感知之外，没有物理上的事件流，没有未来滑入过去的“发生”或“变成”的时刻。</font>

Time, then, is less a universal truth than a feature of how we interact with the world. 
<font color="lightgrey">那么，时间与其说是一个普遍真理，不如说是我们与世界互动方式的一个特征。</font>

What we’re left with, when we release ourselves from the cosmic clock, is “lived time”: our personal, flexible experience of change. 
<font color="lightgrey">当我们从宇宙时钟中解脱出来时，我们剩下的是“生活时间”：我们个人的、灵活的变化体验。</font>

This gives us a different way of thinking about how our lives progress, probably releasing us from the constant beat of the clock. 
<font color="lightgrey">这给了我们一种思考生活进展的不同方式，可能会让我们从时钟不断的节拍中解脱出来。</font>

Lived time is different from clock time because it isn’t defined by a numerical counter chopping moments into seconds. 
<font color="lightgrey">生活时间不同于时钟时间，因为它不是由将时刻切成秒的数字计数器定义的。</font>

It’s a creative process that’s vital not just for our sense of time but of who we are. 
<font color="lightgrey">这是一个创造性的过程，它不仅对我们的时间感至关重要，而且对我们是谁也至关重要。</font>

To tackle time famine, remind yourself that the clock is a tool, not a master. 
<font color="lightgrey">为了应对时间饥荒，提醒自己时钟是一个工具，而不是主人。</font>

**<font color="red">In contrast to</font>** clock time, lived time expands and grows richer the more we focus on it. 
<font color="lightgrey">**与**时钟时间**相比**，我们越关注生活时间，它就越扩张并变得越丰富。</font>

Instead of something we chase and never catch, it’s a flow that connects us with each other. 
<font color="lightgrey">它不是我们追逐却永远抓不住的东西，而是一种将我们彼此联系在一起的流动。</font>

### 【题目详细解析】

**31. Which situation best illustrates “time famine”?**
<font color="lightgrey">哪种情况最能说明“时间饥荒”？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：...the more efficient and productive we try to be, the more precisely we measure time... the less time we actually feel we have. (Para. 3)</font>

*   **A. A busy mother has no time to help her neighbors.**
    *   **错误原因**：虽然提到了“没时间帮邻居”，但这只是时间饥荒的结果之一，而非其核心定义（越追求效率越觉得没时间）。
*   **B. An artist spends hours painting without noticing time.**
    *   **错误原因**：这描述的是“心流”状态，与时间饥荒（感到时间匮乏）相反。
*   **C. A manager who plans every minute always feels behind.**
    *   **正确依据**：原文指出时间饥荒的特征是“the more precisely we measure time... the less time we actually feel we have”（越精确衡量时间，越觉得时间少）。经理“计划每一分钟”对应精确衡量，“总是感到落后”对应感觉时间少。
*   **D. A professional with a packed schedule feels productive.**
    *   **错误原因**：时间饥荒的人通常感到压力和匮乏，而非“多产”的满足感。

**32. What does the author mainly do in Paragraph 4?**
<font color="lightgrey">作者在第4段主要做了什么？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：Our experience of time can vary hugely... in certain circumstances, our sense of time can even go in circles... (Para. 4)</font>

*   **A. Challenge a theory.**
    *   **错误原因**：第4段主要是描述现象，而非直接挑战某个具体的科学理论。
*   **B. Reshape a perspective.**
    *   **错误原因**：这是整篇文章的目的，第4段只是其中的一个环节。
*   **C. Present an assumption.**
    *   **错误原因**：文中描述的是真实的体验和案例，而非假设。
*   **D. Analyze a phenomenon.**
    *   **正确依据**：第4段通过描述时间感的巨大差异（无聊时漫长、兴奋时飞逝）以及Lara的极端案例，分析了时间感并非恒定这一现象，并引出“我们创造了自己的时间”这一深层结论。

**33. What can be inferred about “lived time” from the passage?**
<font color="lightgrey">从文章中可以推断出关于“生活时间”的什么？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：Lived time is different from clock time because it isn’t defined by a numerical counter... It’s a creative process... (Para. 6)</font>

*   **A. It unfolds through experience not calculation.**
    *   **正确依据**：原文明确说生活时间“isn’t defined by a numerical counter”（不是由数字计数器定义的），并且是“our personal, flexible experience of change”（我们个人的、灵活的变化体验）。这说明它依赖于体验而非计算。
*   **B. It replaces clock time in our daily schedules.**
    *   **错误原因**：原文说时钟时间是“mathematical tool... we can no longer get by without it”，说明生活时间无法完全取代时钟时间。
*   **C. More time measurement enriches lived time.**
    *   **错误原因**：与原文相悖。原文说越精确衡量时间（measure time）越会导致时间饥荒，而生活时间是“release ourselves from the cosmic clock”。
*   **D. It is too flexible to be planned or structured.**
    *   **错误原因**：虽然它具有灵活性，但文章并未说它“无法被计划”。

**34. What does the author intend to tell us about time?**
<font color="lightgrey">作者想告诉我们关于时间的什么？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">核心思想：文章旨在破除“时钟时间”是绝对真理的神话，鼓励人们关注“生活时间”，将时间视为与世界互动的工具而非主人。</font>

*   **A. How fast time flows depends on emotion.**
    *   **错误原因**：这只是文中的一个细节，不能概括全篇主旨。
*   **B. Time is a universal truth independent of us.**
    *   **错误原因**：与原文相悖。原文说“Time... is less a universal truth than a feature of how we interact with the world”。
*   **C. Time demands engagement over obedience.**
    *   **正确依据**：文章最后建议“remind yourself that the clock is a tool, not a master”，并强调生活时间是“a flow that connects us with each other”。这说明我们应该积极参与（engagement）创造自己的时间，而不是盲目服从（obedience）时钟。
*   **D. Working more efficiently solves time famine.**
    *   **错误原因**：与原文相悖。原文说越追求效率（efficient）反而越容易产生时间饥荒。
