# 2026年西城区高三二模英语阅读解析（C、D篇）

## 阅读 C 篇

### 【原文逐句对照与考点解析】

What do social climbers and gossipers have in common? 
<font color="lightgrey">钻营社交者和爱说闲话的人有什么共同点？</font>

My mother believes that both are morally suspect, a lesson we readily **<font color="red">pass on to</font>** our children: avoid the cheater and the whisperer. 
<font color="lightgrey">我母亲认为两者在道德上都是可疑的，这是我们很容易**传给**孩子的一个教训：避开骗子和窃窃私语者。</font>

But stories simplify reality. 
<font color="lightgrey">但故事简化了现实。</font>

The most effective social climbers and gossipers possess a remarkable grasp of social structure to **<font color="red">navigate</font>** their social worlds. 
<font color="lightgrey">最有效的社交钻营者和说闲话者对社会结构有着非凡的把握，以便在他们的社交世界中**导航**。</font>

This skill isn’t a moral failing; it’s a cognitive skill. 
<font color="lightgrey">这种技能不是道德上的缺陷；它是一种认知技能。</font>

Recent work from my laboratory shows that cognitive maps—mental representations of the social world—shape our critical social skills. 
<font color="lightgrey">我实验室最近的工作表明，认知地图——社交世界的心理表征——塑造了我们关键的社交技能。</font>

Social success **<font color="red">depends not just on</font>** whom you know **<font color="red">but also on</font>** how well you understand the invisible architecture of your social world. 
<font color="lightgrey">社交成功**不仅取决于**你认识谁，**还取决于**你对社交世界无形架构的理解程度。</font>

Mapping this is no small task, as social networks are large and dynamic. 
<font color="lightgrey">绘制这种地图并非易事，因为社交网络庞大且动态。</font>

Yet building such cognitive maps offers great advantages. 
<font color="lightgrey">然而，构建这样的认知地图提供了巨大的优势。</font>

To better understand social navigation, my collaborator and I developed studies to investigate how people build cognitive maps. 
<font color="lightgrey">为了更好地理解社交导航，我的合作者和我开展了研究，以调查人们如何构建认知地图。</font>

**<font color="red">Across a year</font>**, we tracked about 200 freshmen’s friendships and asked them to report their understanding of others’ connections. 
<font color="lightgrey">**在一年时间里**，我们追踪了大约200名新生的友谊，并要求他们报告对他人联系的理解。</font>

In one study, we discovered that those who rise to the top of the social structure aren’t the most charming or outgoing—they’re the best social mapmakers. 
<font color="lightgrey">在一项研究中，我们发现那些上升到社会结构顶层的人并不是最迷人或最外向的——他们是最好的社交地图绘制者。</font>

The most influential people quickly build mental maps of their peers’ connection; those initially influential but without accurate mental maps of the network did not **<font color="red">stay influential for long</font>**. 
<font color="lightgrey">最有影响力的人能迅速建立同伴联系的心理地图；那些最初有影响力但没有准确网络心理地图的人，其**影响力并没能持续很久**。</font>

In a second paper, we examined whether mapmaking aids gossiping—a behavior that, despite its poor reputation, can be an efficient way to quickly learn about the **<font color="red">ins and outs</font>** of the community. 
<font color="lightgrey">在第二篇论文中，我们研究了地图绘制是否辅助说闲话——这种行为尽管名声不佳，但却是快速了解社区**详情**的一种有效方式。</font>

To understand how humans **<font color="red">pull off</font>** this remarkable task, we wondered whether mapmaking helps predict where information will spread. 
<font color="lightgrey">为了理解人类如何**完成**这项非凡的任务，我们想知道地图绘制是否有助于预测信息将传播到何处。</font>

Mental maps become quite useful in this case, as they reveal two key network features: someone’s popularity and their distance from the gossip target. 
<font color="lightgrey">在这种情况下，心理地图变得非常有用，因为它们揭示了两个关键的网络特征：某人的受欢迎程度以及他们与八卦目标的距离。</font>

They help find a good friend—someone far enough from the target yet **<font color="red">well-connected</font>** to spread information. 
<font color="lightgrey">它们有助于找到一个好朋友——一个离目标足够远但又**人脉广泛**、足以传播信息的人。</font>

How does the brain build these maps? 
<font color="lightgrey">大脑是如何构建这些地图的？</font>

In one of our recent studies, we discovered that the hippocampus and entorhinal cortex—a neural center known for navigating physical space—also carries a map of connections among people. 
<font color="lightgrey">在我们最近的一项研究中，我们发现海马体和内嗅皮层——一个以导航物理空间而闻名的神经中心——也携带了一张人与人之间联系的地图。</font>

The more strongly these maps are **<font color="red">embedded in</font>** the brain, the better we are at **<font color="red">brokering</font>** community ties. 
<font color="lightgrey">这些地图在大脑中**嵌入**得越牢固，我们就越擅长**协调**社区关系。</font>

If the brain needs to quickly **<font color="red">figure out</font>** where gossip might spread, knowing where the popular people are positioned or the key relationships that bridge the otherwise disconnected communities allows us to chart the sequence of ties that can efficiently cross the network. 
<font color="lightgrey">如果大脑需要快速**弄清楚**八卦可能传播到哪里，了解受欢迎的人所处的位置，或者连接原本断开的社区的关键关系，就能让我们绘制出能够有效跨越网络的联系序列。</font>

Strategic wayfinding isn’t only for physical space. 
<font color="lightgrey">战略性寻路不仅适用于物理空间。</font>

It is just as necessary to be able to effectively move through our social landscapes. 
<font color="lightgrey">能够有效地穿梭于我们的社交景观中同样必要。</font>

Armed with a deliberately unclear map of their social community, skilled social navigators can do what no GPS can. 
<font color="lightgrey">凭借对其社交社区一张刻意模糊的地图，熟练的社交导航员可以做到GPS做不到的事情。</font>

They see the bridges before they’re built, avoid the storms of gossip, and map out a course to **<font color="red">common ground</font>**. 
<font color="lightgrey">他们在桥梁建成前就能预见，避开八卦风暴，并绘制出通往**共同点**的路线。</font>

### 【题目详细解析】

**28. As for his mother’s belief, the author is ________.**
<font color="lightgrey">对于他母亲的信念，作者的态度是怎样的？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：My mother believes that both are morally suspect... But stories simplify reality. (Para. 1-2)</font>

*   **A. disapproving**
    *   **正确依据**：作者在第一段提到母亲认为社交钻营者和说闲话者在道德上可疑，紧接着在第二段用“But”转折，指出“stories simplify reality”（故事简化了现实），并论证这其实是一种认知技能（cognitive skill）。这表明作者并不认同母亲单纯从道德层面进行否定的看法。
*   **B. supportive**
    *   **错误原因**：与原文转折逻辑相悖。作者是在挑战母亲的传统观点。
*   **C. puzzled**
    *   **错误原因**：无中生有。作者对这一现象有深入的研究和清晰的解释，并不感到困惑。
*   **D. unconcerned**
    *   **错误原因**：词义不符。作者专门对此进行了实验室研究，说明他非常关注这一话题。

**29. According to the passage, social cognitive maps can help ________.**
<font color="lightgrey">根据文章，社交认知地图可以帮助做什么？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：The most influential people quickly build mental maps... those initially influential but without accurate mental maps... did not stay influential for long. (Para. 4)</font>

*   **A. control the wide spread of gossip storms**
    *   **错误原因**：曲解原意。文中提到的是“avoid the storms of gossip”（避开八卦风暴）或预测传播，而非“控制”其大规模传播。
*   **B. memorize others’ connections in social groups**
    *   **错误原因**：表述浅层。认知地图不仅是“记忆”，更是一种对架构的“理解”和“表征”，用于导航。
*   **C. gain long-lasting social influence among peers**
    *   **正确依据**：原文指出最有影响力的人能迅速建立心理地图，而没有准确地图的人“did not stay influential for long”（影响力不能持久）。反推可知，准确的认知地图有助于获得持久的社交影响力。
*   **D. choose well-connected people as gossip targets**
    *   **错误原因**：张冠李戴。文中提到的是找人“传播”信息，而非将人选为“目标”。

**30. What does the underlined word “brokering” in Paragraph 7 probably mean?**
<font color="lightgrey">第7段中带下划线的词“brokering”最可能是什么意思？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：The more strongly these maps are embedded in the brain, the better we are at brokering community ties. (Para. 7)</font>

*   **A. Breaking.**
    *   **错误原因**：词义相反。文章讨论的是建立和利用联系，而非破坏联系。
*   **B. Managing.**
    *   **正确依据**：根据上下文，认知地图越强，就越擅长处理、协调或管理（managing）社区中的各种关系（ties）。“broker”原意为经纪人、中间人，在此语境下指协调和处理关系。
*   **C. Experiencing.**
    *   **错误原因**：词义过轻。这里强调的是一种主动的技能，而非被动的体验。
*   **D. Recording.**
    *   **错误原因**：词义不全。记录只是建立地图的过程，而“brokering ties”侧重于对这些关系的实际应用和处理。

**31. Which would be the best title for the passage?**
<font color="lightgrey">这篇文章最好的标题是什么？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">核心思想：文章探讨了人类如何通过大脑中的“认知地图”来理解和穿梭于复杂的社交网络中，将这种能力比作社交导航。</font>

*   **A. How to Interpret Social Skills**
    *   **错误原因**：范围过大。文章特指“认知地图”这一具体的社交技能。
*   **B. Navigating Our Social Worlds**
    *   **正确依据**：文章多次提到“social navigation”、“navigate their social worlds”、“social landscapes”，并以GPS和寻路为类比，完美概括了利用认知地图在社交世界中“导航”的主旨。
*   **C. What Lies Behind People’s Social Behaviors**
    *   **错误原因**：过于笼统。虽然认知地图是行为背后的原因，但标题没有体现出“导航”和“地图”这两个核心意象。
*   **D. Brain Structures Shaping Social Relationships**
    *   **错误原因**：以偏概全。虽然提到了海马体等大脑结构，但这只是为了解释认知地图的生理基础，而非全文核心。

## 阅读 D 篇

### 【原文逐句对照与考点解析】

Knowledge is dead. 
<font color="lightgrey">知识已死。</font>

Not in the sense that truth has disappeared or that learning no longer matters, but in the deeper, structural sense that knowledge, as a stable possession has lost its central role in human cognition. 
<font color="lightgrey">这并不是说真理消失了，或者学习不再重要，而是在更深层的结构意义上，知识作为一种稳定的占有物，已经失去了它在人类认知中的核心地位。</font>

In a world where information is instant and increasingly available “**<font color="red">on demand</font>**”, the old idea of “knowing” seems to feel like an antique of another era. 
<font color="lightgrey">在一个信息即时且越来越可以“**按需**”获取的世界里，“知道”的旧观念似乎成了另一个时代的古董。</font>

Artificial intelligence has also changed the structure of cognition itself. 
<font color="lightgrey">人工智能也改变了认知本身的结构。</font>

Understanding now **<font color="red">unfolds as</font>** an iterative process rather than a final state. 
<font color="lightgrey">理解现在**展现为**一个迭代的过程，而不是一个最终的状态。</font>

We iterate facts and ideas that “collapse the information function” into a construct that, in some instances, has never existed. 
<font color="lightgrey">我们迭代事实和想法，将“信息功能”折叠成一个在某些情况下从未存在过的构造。</font>

Insight emerges through cycles, not conclusions, as knowledge changes from fixed maps to dynamic webs. 
<font color="lightgrey">洞察力通过循环而非结论产生，因为知识从固定的地图变成了动态的网络。</font>

Alongside this shift comes the collapse of academic monovision. 
<font color="lightgrey">伴随着这一转变，学术上的单一视角也随之瓦解。</font>

Human perception, statistical inference, interpretive meaning, moral judgment, and machine-generated pattern recognition now occupy the same cognitive field. 
<font color="lightgrey">人类感知、统计推断、解释性意义、道德判断和机器生成的模式识别现在占据了同一个认知领域。</font>

No one perspective is sufficient **<font color="red">on its own</font>** and depth arises from the perspective of multiple frames and learning to move among them. 
<font color="lightgrey">没有任何一种视角**本身**是足够的，深度产生于多重框架的视角以及学习在它们之间切换。</font>

Education, however, is still largely organized **<font color="red">in the context of</font>** that fixed map. 
<font color="lightgrey">然而，教育在很大程度上仍然是**在**那种固定地图的**背景下**组织的。</font>

Subjects are separated as if the world presented itself in disciplinary divisions. 
<font color="lightgrey">学科被分开，就好像世界是以学科划分的形式呈现的一样。</font>

Mastery is assessed as if memorization were a reliable indicator of understanding. 
<font color="lightgrey">精通程度的评估就好像记忆是理解的可靠指标一样。</font>

This is where the idea of the learning studio becomes more than an educational experiment. 
<font color="lightgrey">这就是学习工作室的理念不再仅仅是一个教育实验的地方。</font>

A studio isn’t defined by a single discipline, but by a question complex enough to **<font color="red">demand</font>** many. 
<font color="lightgrey">工作室不是由单一学科定义的，而是由一个复杂到足以**需要**多个学科的问题定义的。</font>

Think about a studio centered on the biology of aging. 
<font color="lightgrey">想想一个以衰老生物学为中心的实验室。</font>

Cellular mechanisms, statistical modeling, moral questions of longevity, and the social implications of demographic change would gather in a single cognitive space. 
<font color="lightgrey">细胞机制、统计建模、长寿的道德问题以及人口变化的社会影响将聚集在同一个认知空间中。</font>

In these settings, students wouldn’t move from class to class so much as move through cognitive environments. 
<font color="lightgrey">在这些环境中，学生与其说是从一个班级移动到另一个班级，不如说是穿梭于不同的认知环境。</font>

Science, mathematics, humanities, and computation would no longer be neighboring divisions but interdependent ways of **<font color="red">making sense of</font>** a shared problem. 
<font color="lightgrey">科学、数学、人文和计算将不再是相邻的分支，而是相互依存的**理解**共同问题的方式。</font>

Technology and AI wouldn’t be present as tools of efficiency, but also as thinking partners that drive human achievement. 
<font color="lightgrey">技术和AI将不仅作为效率工具存在，还将作为推动人类成就的思考伙伴。</font>

Re-architecting education in this way also reframes its ultimate purpose—and this is the most critical point. 
<font color="lightgrey">以这种方式重新构建教育也重塑了它的最终目的——这是最关键的一点。</font>

The traditional endpoint has been certification or the preparation for the next stage. 
<font color="lightgrey">传统的终点一直是获得证书或为下一阶段做准备。</font>

In a world where knowledge is dynamic and AI increasingly competent, that endpoint begins to look insufficient. 
<font color="lightgrey">在一个知识是动态的且AI越来越有能力的世界里，那个终点开始显得不足。</font>

What becomes more convincing is the idea of cognitive sovereignty. 
<font color="lightgrey">变得更有说服力的是认知主权的概念。</font>

This is the capacity to remain the author of one’s own understanding **<font color="red">in the presence of</font>** an overload of information and persuasive technologies. 
<font color="lightgrey">这是在信息过载和具有说服力的技术**面前**，保持自己理解力的主导地位的能力。</font>

For teenagers, this is not an abstract philosophical goal; it’s developmental. 
<font color="lightgrey">对于青少年来说，这不仅是一个抽象的哲学目标；它是发展性的。</font>

The teenage years are when abstract reasoning and even identity are forming. 
<font color="lightgrey">青少年时期是抽象推理甚至身份形成的时候。</font>

An education that immerses students in integration and iteration can cultivate something more durable. 
<font color="lightgrey">一种让学生沉浸在整合和迭代中的教育可以培养出更持久的东西。</font>

Simply put, it builds a mind capable of **<font color="red">handling uncertainty</font>**. 
<font color="lightgrey">简单来说，它建立了一个能够**处理不确定性**的头脑。</font>

### 【题目详细解析】

**32. Which of the following is a core feature of the learning studio?**
<font color="lightgrey">以下哪项是学习工作室的核心特征？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：A studio isn’t defined by a single discipline, but by a question complex enough to demand many... Science, mathematics, humanities, and computation would no longer be neighboring divisions but interdependent ways... (Para. 5)</font>

*   **A. The intersection of multiple domains.**
    *   **正确依据**：原文指出工作室不是由单一学科定义的，而是由需要多个学科（demand many）的复杂问题定义的。科学、数学、人文等不再是分开的，而是“interdependent ways”（相互依存的方式）。这正是“多领域交叉”的体现。
*   **B. The use of AI as productivity assistants.**
    *   **错误原因**：表述不全。原文说AI不仅是效率工具（tools of efficiency），还是“thinking partners”（思考伙伴）。
*   **C. The shift between physical environments.**
    *   **错误原因**：词义误解。原文说的是穿梭于“cognitive environments”（认知环境），而非物理环境。
*   **D. The focus on preparing students for the future.**
    *   **错误原因**：过于笼统。这是所有教育的共同目标，不能体现“学习工作室”的独特核心特征。

**33. What can be learned from the passage?**
<font color="lightgrey">从文章中可以学到什么？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：What becomes more convincing is the idea of cognitive sovereignty. This is the capacity to remain the author of one’s own understanding... (Para. 6)</font>

*   **A. Education should aim for autonomy of thought.**
    *   **正确依据**：原文提出了“cognitive sovereignty”（认知主权）的概念，并将其定义为“remain the author of one’s own understanding”（保持自己理解力的主导地位）。这本质上就是追求思想的自主性（autonomy of thought）。
*   **B. Abstract reasoning is key to forming an identity.**
    *   **错误原因**：因果倒置或表述不准。原文说青少年时期是抽象推理和身份“正在形成”的时候，并未说前者是后者的“关键”。
*   **C. Information accessibility facilitates knowledge webs.**
    *   **错误原因**：逻辑偏差。原文说知识变成了动态网络（dynamic webs）是认知结构改变的结果，而信息即时获取（accessibility）使得旧的“知道”观念过时。
*   **D. Understanding is attained through cycles of repetition.**
    *   **错误原因**：词义误解。原文说理解是“iterative process”（迭代过程）和“cycles”（循环），而非简单的“repetition”（重复）。迭代意味着在循环中不断进化。

**34. What does the author mainly do in this passage?**
<font color="lightgrey">作者在文章中主要做了什么？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">核心逻辑：文章开头指出传统知识观和教育模式的局限（问题），随后提出了“学习工作室”这一新型教育模式（方案），并论证了其必要性和最终目的。这是一种典型的“倡导新模式”的写法。</font>

*   **A. Analyze current challenges.**
    *   **错误原因**：只是文章的一部分。分析挑战是为了引出新模式。
*   **B. Illustrate opposing ideas.**
    *   **错误原因**：文章虽然对比了新旧模式，但重点在于推介新模式，而非单纯展示对立观点。
*   **C. Question an old practice.**
    *   **错误原因**：只是手段。质疑旧的学科划分是为了倡导新的整合模式。
*   **D. Advocate a novel model.**
    *   **正确依据**：通篇来看，作者在倡导一种基于“学习工作室”的、跨学科整合的、注重认知主权的教育新模式（novel model）。
