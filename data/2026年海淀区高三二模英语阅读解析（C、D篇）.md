# 2026年海淀区高三二模英语阅读解析（C、D篇）

## 阅读 C 篇

### 【原文逐句对照与考点解析】

Artists enjoy calling their work “original”. 
<font color="lightgrey">艺术家们喜欢称他们的作品为“原创”。</font>

For good reason too, as artists, we want to **<font color="red">distinguish ourselves</font>** and **<font color="red">convey a genuine message</font>** to the world. 
<font color="lightgrey">这也是有充分理由的，作为艺术家，我们想要**使我们自己与众不同**，并向世界**传达一个真实的信息**。</font>

But there are hardly any truly original ideas in the art world. 
<font color="lightgrey">但在艺术界几乎没有任何真正原创的想法。</font>

Everything has been invented, reinvented, and re-reinvented over again. 
<font color="lightgrey">一切都已经被发明、重新发明，并一再地被重新发明。</font>

Recently, I have been reflecting on how I get ideas for my work as a professional photographer. 
<font color="lightgrey">最近，我一直在反思作为一名职业摄影师，我是如何为我的作品获取灵感的。</font>

Some of my images can **<font color="red">be traced back directly to</font>** what inspired them, while some others are more “unique”. 
<font color="lightgrey">我的一些图像可以**直接追溯到**激发它们的灵感来源，而另一些则更具“独特性”。</font>

Nevertheless, I cannot genuinely **<font color="red">classify</font>** any piece of my output **<font color="red">as</font>** truly original, since every image ultimately represents a synthesized fusion of previous visual influences, external conceptual inputs from others, and **<font color="red">a diverse range of</font>** personal life experiences. 
<font color="lightgrey">尽管如此，我无法真诚地将我的任何作品**归类为**真正的原创，因为每张图像最终都代表了先前视觉影响、来自他人的外部概念输入以及**各种各样的**个人生活经历的综合融合。</font>

This personal realization prompts a deeper and more universal inquiry into the nature of originality. 
<font color="lightgrey">这种个人的感悟引发了对原创性本质的更深层且更普遍的探究。</font>

If we **<font color="red">go by the principle</font>** that all ideas are a hodgepodge of other ideas, a natural question arises. 
<font color="lightgrey">如果我们**遵循**所有想法都是其他想法的大杂烩这一**原则**，一个自然的问题就产生了。</font>

Is it possible to **<font color="red">trace back</font>** and find the original ideas? 
<font color="lightgrey">是否有可能**追溯**并找到最初的想法？</font>

The first original thought must have been by the first man. 
<font color="lightgrey">第一个原创想法一定是由第一个人产生的。</font>

Ever since, it has all been one big fake. 
<font color="lightgrey">从那以后，一切都成了一个巨大的伪装。</font>

This definition will take us exactly nowhere, and the debate will end right here. 
<font color="lightgrey">这个定义将使我们一事无成，争论也将在此结束。</font>

So, let’s stop hunting for an original idea, **<font color="red">whatever it may mean</font>**. 
<font color="lightgrey">所以，让我们停止寻找原创想法吧，**无论它意味着什么**。</font>

Just think how some fashion brands **<font color="red">manage to produce</font>** two new couture collections each year. 
<font color="lightgrey">想想一些时尚品牌是如何**设法**每年**生产**出两个新的高级定制系列的。</font>

Fashion has **<font color="red">mastered the art of</font>** showing old as new, and new as old. 
<font color="lightgrey">时尚已经**精通了**将旧的展示为新的、将新的展示为旧的**艺术**。</font>

The approach to originality that I suggest is that all ideas are a byproduct of other ideas, and what truly matters is the intention you hold behind those ideas. 
<font color="lightgrey">我建议的对待原创性的方法是，所有的想法都是其他想法的副产品，而真正重要的是你这些想法背后的意图。</font>

If you **<font color="red">take inspiration from</font>** Rodin’s work and see people in a similarly monumental way, there is meaning in the work and you are creating something authentic, something that is authentic and **<font color="red">true to you</font>**. 
<font color="lightgrey">如果你**从**罗丹的作品中**获得灵感**，并以同样宏伟的方式看待人们，那么作品中就有意义，你正在创造一些真实的东西，一些对你来说真实且**忠于自我**的东西。</font>

But, if your intention is to copy Rodin’s work because it made him a famous sculptor, your work will have very little meaning and authenticity. 
<font color="lightgrey">但是，如果你的意图是因为罗丹的作品使他成为著名的雕塑家而抄袭他的作品，那么你的作品将几乎没有意义和真实性。</font>

The point I am trying to make about authenticity is that it doesn’t have to be authentic to anybody else but you. 
<font color="lightgrey">关于真实性，我想表达的一点是，它不必对除了你之外的任何人真实。</font>

If we take out the obvious instances where one artist copied another artist to **<font color="red">make money off</font>** the idea, we are left with a world of trillions of thoughts and ideas, each ready to inspire you to create something that is authentic to you, something that **<font color="red">speaks to you</font>**. 
<font color="lightgrey">如果我们排除那些明显的艺术家抄袭另一位艺术家以从该创意中**赚钱**的例子，我们剩下的就是一个拥有数万亿思想和想法的世界，每一个都准备好激励你创造出对你来说真实的东西，一些**引起你共鸣**的东西。</font>

Even if you take pictures only because they “look beautiful”, they already mean something to you: they mean beauty. 
<font color="lightgrey">即使你拍照片只是因为它们“看起来很美”，它们对你来说已经有了意义：它们意味着美。</font>

### 【题目详细解析】

**28. What can be inferred about the writer’s photographing experience?**
<font color="lightgrey">关于作者的摄影经历可以推断出什么？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：Nevertheless, I cannot genuinely classify any piece of my output as truly original, since every image ultimately represents a synthesized fusion of previous visual influences, external conceptual inputs from others, and a diverse range of personal life experiences. (Para. 2)</font>

*   **A. He considers his works truly original.**
    *   **错误原因**：与原文相悖。原文明确提到“I cannot genuinely classify any piece of my output as truly original”（我无法真诚地将我的任何作品归类为真正的原创）。
*   **B. He insists on pursuing original creation.**
    *   **错误原因**：过度推断。作者在文中建议“let’s stop hunting for an original idea”（让我们停止寻找原创想法），他更强调的是“intention”（意图）而非形式上的原创。
*   **C. He traces all photos to direct sources.**
    *   **错误原因**：表述过于绝对。原文说“Some of my images can be traced back directly... while some others are more ‘unique’”（我的一些图像可以直接追溯……而另一些则更具“独特性”），并非“all”（所有）。
*   **D. He admits combining various influences.**
    *   **正确依据**：原文指出每张图像代表了“a synthesized fusion of previous visual influences, external conceptual inputs from others, and a diverse range of personal life experiences”（先前视觉影响、他人外部概念输入和各种个人生活经历的综合融合）。“synthesized fusion”对应选项中的“combining”，“various influences”对应文中的多种来源。

**29. What does the underlined word “hodgepodge” in Paragraph 3 most probably mean?**
<font color="lightgrey">第3段中带下划线的词“hodgepodge”最可能是什么意思？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：If we go by the principle that all ideas are a hodgepodge of other ideas, a natural question arises. (Para. 3)</font>

*   **A. Mixture.**
    *   **正确依据**：根据上下文，作者认为所有的想法都是由其他想法融合而成的（synthesized fusion），因此“hodgepodge”在这里表示“大杂烩、混合物”。
*   **B. Share.**
    *   **错误原因**：词义不符。“Share”意为“分享、份额”，无法体现想法之间融合、杂乱组合的状态。
*   **C. Revision.**
    *   **错误原因**：词义不符。“Revision”意为“修订、修改”，虽然文中提到“reinvented”，但此处强调的是想法的构成成分。
*   **D. Repetition.**
    *   **错误原因**：词义不符。“Repetition”意为“重复”，文中虽然说一切都被一再发明，但“hodgepodge of other ideas”侧重于多种想法的混合。

**30. What might the author agree with?**
<font color="lightgrey">作者可能同意什么观点？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：The approach to originality that I suggest is that all ideas are a byproduct of other ideas, and what truly matters is the intention you hold behind those ideas. (Para. 4)</font>

*   **A. Authenticity stems from originality.**
    *   **错误原因**：逻辑颠倒。作者认为几乎没有真正的“originality”，但可以有“authenticity”，两者并非因果关系。
*   **B. There is no such thing as authenticity.**
    *   **错误原因**：与原文相悖。作者明确提到“you are creating something authentic”（你正在创造一些真实的东西）。
*   **C. Authenticity is defined by intentions.**
    *   **正确依据**：原文提到“what truly matters is the intention you hold behind those ideas... you are creating something authentic”（真正重要的是你这些想法背后的意图……你正在创造一些真实的东西）。作者认为意图决定了作品是否真实。
*   **D. Originality rests on objective standards.**
    *   **错误原因**：无中生有。文中并未提到原创性的“客观标准”，反而强调了主观的意图和个人体验。

## 阅读 D 篇

### 【原文逐句对照与考点解析】

The most dangerous myths are the ones we don’t see. 
<font color="lightgrey">最危险的神话是我们看不见的那些。</font>

Human exceptionalism — the belief that humans are fundamentally **<font color="red">superior to</font>** the rest of nature — is one of those myths. 
<font color="lightgrey">人类例外论——即认为人类从根本上**优于**自然界其他部分的信念——就是这些神话之一。</font>

This worldview is not hidden because it’s obscure — it’s hidden because it’s everywhere, **<font color="red">taken for granted</font>**, and rarely questioned. 
<font color="lightgrey">这种世界观之所以没有被隐藏，并不是因为它晦涩难懂——它被隐藏是因为它无处不在，被**认为理所当然**，且很少受到质疑。</font>

But what struck me most is just how thoroughly this belief has infiltrated science — an institution meant to challenge our biases, not reinforce them. 
<font color="lightgrey">但最令我震惊的是，这种信念竟然如此彻底地渗透到了科学中——科学本应是一个挑战我们偏见而非强化偏见的机构。</font>

Take research on self-awareness. 
<font color="lightgrey">以自我意识的研究为例。</font>

For decades, we believed only humans and certain primate species could recognize themselves in mirrors, a supposed benchmark of self-awareness. 
<font color="lightgrey">几十年来，我们认为只有人类和某些灵长类物种能在镜子中识别自己，这被认为是自我意识的一个基准。</font>

But the mirror test **<font color="red">is biased toward</font>** vision. 
<font color="lightgrey">但镜子测试**对**视觉**有偏见**。</font>

Dogs experience the world primarily via scent. 
<font color="lightgrey">狗主要通过嗅觉体验世界。</font>

They pass the smell-based mirror test with ease — demonstrating self-awareness in their dominant sense. 
<font color="lightgrey">它们轻松通过了基于嗅觉的镜子测试——在它们的主导感官中展示了自我意识。</font>

When we measure the world with a human-oriented ruler, other species inevitably **<font color="red">come up short</font>**. 
<font color="lightgrey">当我们用以人为本的尺子衡量世界时，其他物种不可避免地会**显得不足**。</font>

Many **<font color="red">treat</font>** human exceptionalism **<font color="red">as</font>** a natural conclusion. 
<font color="lightgrey">许多人将人类例外论**视为**一个自然的结论。</font>

But recent studies in developmental and cross-cultural psychology suggest otherwise. 
<font color="lightgrey">但最近在发展心理学和跨文化心理学方面的研究表明事实并非如此。</font>

Beliefs in human exceptionalism aren’t an inevitably biological outcome — they instead reflect a cultural worldview. 
<font color="lightgrey">对人类例外论的信仰并非必然的生物学结果——相反，它们反映了一种文化世界观。</font>

Across repeated studies, when **<font color="red">presented with</font>** moral dilemmas — such as saving one human or multiple animals — adults overwhelmingly **<font color="red">favored</font>** humans **<font color="red">over</font>** multiple animals, even when the trade-off involved 100 dogs or pigs. 
<font color="lightgrey">在重复的研究中，当**面对**道德困境时——比如救一个人还是救多只动物——成年人压倒性地**比起**多只动物**更偏好**人类，即使这种权衡涉及100只狗或猪。</font>

Children, however, often chose to save multiple animals over one human, valuing human and nonhuman lives far more similarly. 
<font color="lightgrey">然而，孩子们通常选择救多只动物而不是一个人，他们对人类和非人类生命的价值评估要相似得多。</font>

This suggests that the human-centred moral frameworks commonly held by adults are not the biological default, but **<font color="red">emerge over time</font>** through cultural learning — particularly as children become increasingly **<font color="red">exposed to</font>** the ways other forms of life are used and valued in our society. 
<font color="lightgrey">这表明成年人普遍持有的以人为中心的道德框架并非生物学上的默认设置，而是通过文化学习**随着时间的推移而出现**的——特别是随着孩子们越来越多地**接触到**我们社会中其他生命形式被使用和估值的方式。</font>

Research across human cultures also reveals that human exceptionalism is far from universal. 
<font color="lightgrey">跨人类文化的研究还显示，人类例外论远非普遍存在。</font>

Many Indigenous and non-Western knowledge systems reject such natural hierarchies. 
<font color="lightgrey">许多土著和非西方知识体系拒绝这种自然等级制度。</font>

They recognize other animals, plants, rivers, forests, and mountains as kin: sentient, agentive beings embedded in a shared moral and ecological world. 
<font color="lightgrey">他们将其他动物、植物、河流、森林和山脉视为亲属：嵌入在共同道德和生态世界中的有感知、有能动性的存在。</font>

Within these frameworks, the notion that humans are separate or superior simply doesn’t hold. 
<font color="lightgrey">在这些框架内，人类是独立的或优越的这一观念根本站不住脚。</font>

While writing my last book, I was introduced to various alternative cosmologies that reject the ideology of human exceptionalism. 
<font color="lightgrey">在写我上一本书时，我接触到了各种拒绝人类例外论意识形态的替代宇宙观。</font>

These worldviews model ways of living **<font color="red">in greater balance with</font>** the rest of the natural world. 
<font color="lightgrey">这些世界观模拟了与自然界其他部分**更加平衡**的生活方式。</font>

Some today maintain that humans are the most evolutionarily “successful” species. 
<font color="lightgrey">今天有些人坚持认为人类是进化上最“成功”的物种。</font>

Success, in this view, is **<font color="red">measured by</font>** ecological dominance. 
<font color="lightgrey">在这种观点中，成功是**由**生态主导地位来**衡量的**。</font>

But in reality, the most resilient ecosystems are **<font color="red">built on</font>** interdependence. 
<font color="lightgrey">但在现实中，最强大的生态系统是**建立在**相互依赖之上的。</font>

We’ve constructed our scientific models around struggle and individualism, even though life on earth is **<font color="red">held together by</font>** relationships and co-evolution. 
<font color="lightgrey">我们围绕斗争和个人主义构建了我们的科学模型，尽管地球上的生命是由关系和共同进化**维系在一起的**。</font>

The real insight comes from humility. 
<font color="lightgrey">真正的洞察力来自谦逊。</font>

Seeing ourselves clearly — not as rulers, but as participants in a larger web — is one of the most urgent scientific and moral challenges of our time. 
<font color="lightgrey">清晰地看待我们自己——不是作为统治者，而是作为更大网络中的参与者——是我们这个时代最紧迫的科学和道德挑战之一。</font>

### 【题目详细解析】

**31. The author mentions the mirror test to show that ________.**
<font color="lightgrey">作者提到镜子测试是为了说明什么？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：But the mirror test is biased toward vision... When we measure the world with a human-oriented ruler, other species inevitably come up short. (Para. 2)</font>

*   **A. good science requires repeated tests**
    *   **错误原因**：张冠李戴。虽然科学确实需要重复测试，但作者提到镜子测试是为了说明测试标准本身存在偏见。
*   **B. faulty scientific standards deepen bias**
    *   **正确依据**：原文指出镜子测试“is biased toward vision”（对视觉有偏见），并总结说当我们用“human-oriented ruler”（以人为本的尺子）衡量世界时，其他物种会显得不足。这说明错误的、以人为中心的科学标准加深了对其他物种的偏见。
*   **C. human tests often favor visual senses**
    *   **错误原因**：以偏概全。这只是镜子测试的具体缺陷，作者的目的是通过这个例子说明更广泛的“human exceptionalism”偏见。
*   **D. cognitive tests are invalid for non-primates**
    *   **错误原因**：表述绝对且错误。原文说狗通过了基于嗅觉的测试，说明认知测试并非对非灵长类无效，而是需要合适的标准。

**32. What leads to the belief of human exceptionalism?**
<font color="lightgrey">是什么导致了人类例外论的信念？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：This suggests that the human-centred moral frameworks... emerge over time through cultural learning — particularly as children become increasingly exposed to the ways other forms of life are used and valued in our society. (Para. 4)</font>

*   **A. Human nature.**
    *   **错误原因**：与原文相悖。原文明确说“aren’t an inevitably biological outcome”（并非必然的生物学结果），即不是天性。
*   **B. Knowledge system.**
    *   **错误原因**：范围过窄。虽然知识体系包含其中，但原文强调的是更广泛的文化学习过程。
*   **C. Living area.**
    *   **错误原因**：无中生有。文中未提到居住区域对这一信念的影响。
*   **D. Cultural exposure.**
    *   **正确依据**：原文提到这一信念是通过“cultural learning”（文化学习）产生的，特别是当孩子们“exposed to”（接触到）社会对生命价值的衡量方式时。这对应了“Cultural exposure”。

**33. The author mainly suggests ________.**
<font color="lightgrey">作者主要建议什么？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">原文定位：Seeing ourselves clearly — not as rulers, but as participants in a larger web — is one of the most urgent scientific and moral challenges of our time. (Para. 7)</font>

*   **A. reevaluating human-nature relationship**
    *   **正确依据**：文章通篇在挑战“人类优于自然”的观点，最后一段明确提出要清晰地看待自己，不是作为统治者而是参与者。这本质上就是建议重新评估人类与自然的关系。
*   **B. exploring interdependence across species**
    *   **错误原因**：属于细节。虽然文中提到了相互依赖（interdependence），但这是为了支持重新评估人类地位这一大论点。
*   **C. acknowledging the limitation of science**
    *   **错误原因**：属于论据。作者提到科学被偏见渗透是为了引出人类例外论的问题，而非文章的核心建议。
*   **D. rejecting the idea of evolutionary success**
    *   **错误原因**：表述不准确。作者是挑战以“生态主导”定义成功的观点，而非全盘否定进化成功。

**34. Which would be the best title for the passage?**
<font color="lightgrey">这篇文章最好的标题是什么？</font>
<font color="lightgrey">核心思想：文章主要讨论“人类例外论”并非自然或生物必然，而是一种文化偏见，并呼吁人类放下统治者的姿态，回归自然网络。</font>

*   **A. Are Humans a Successful Species?**
    *   **错误原因**：偏离主题。文章只在倒数第二段提到了成功，并非全篇核心。
*   **B. Putting Humans First Is Not Natural**
    *   **正确依据**：文章核心论点是“human exceptionalism”不是“biological default”（生物默认），而是文化产物。标题“以人类为先并非自然”精准概括了这一核心。
*   **C. What Makes Humans Exceptional?**
    *   **错误原因**：误导性。文章是在否定人类是“exceptional”的，而不是在解释什么使人类特殊。
*   **D. Human Exceptionalism: A Global View**
    *   **错误原因**：范围过大。虽然提到了跨文化研究，但文章更多是在进行科学和道德层面的反思，而非单纯的全球现状综述。
